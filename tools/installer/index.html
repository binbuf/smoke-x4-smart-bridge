Smoke X4 Smart Bridge

Turn a Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V3 into a recorder for your ThermoWorks Smoke X4 — every cook logged to flash, live on your phone, alarms that work with the phone switched off.

Fit the antenna before you power the board. The SX1262 radio can be damaged by transmitting into an open connector. The antenna ships in the box; screw it on first, then plug in USB.
Plug a Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V3 into USB, then: Your browser can't talk to serial ports. Use a recent desktop Chrome, Edge, Brave, or Opera — or flash by hand with the command below. This page has to be served over HTTPS to reach a serial port.

A fresh install will erase everything on the board — including any cooks already stored on it. The installer asks before it does. If you are updating a bridge you already use, do it over Wi-Fi from the app instead; that keeps your history.

After it flashes: first boot

  1. The OLED shows the version and a three-second countdown, then the status pages.
  2. The bridge starts its own Wi-Fi network and advertises over Bluetooth as SmokeBridge-XXXX, where XXXX is the last four characters of its MAC — the same four the screen shows.
  3. Open the Smoke Bridge app and look for SmokeBridge-XXXX. It will show you a six-digit code on the bridge's own screen to pair with, then walk you through joining your Wi-Fi.
  4. Put the Smoke X4 base station into sync mode and let the bridge find it. Your original ThermoWorks receiver keeps working throughout — the bridge only listens.

What you need

  • A Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V3 (ESP32-S3 + SX1262), with its antenna.
  • A ThermoWorks Smoke X4 (or X2) base station.
  • A USB-C cable that carries data — not a charge-only one.
  • An Android phone for the app. iOS is not supported yet.

Flashing by hand

The installer above does exactly this, in a browser. If you would rather do it yourself, download smoke-bridge-heltec-v3-<version>.bin from the releases page and run: 

python -m esptool --chip esp32s3 -p <PORT> -b 460800 \
    write_flash 0x0 smoke-bridge-heltec-v3-<version>.bin

That file is the merged image: bootloader, partition table, OTA selector and application in one, flashable at offset 0. The other release artifact, smoke-bridge-<version>-ota.bin, is the application alone and is what the app sends over Wi-Fi when you update. They are not interchangeable — the bridge will refuse the merged image over the air, and by hand at offset 0 the OTA image will not boot.

Source, licence, privacy

Everything is on GitHub under the MIT licence, including the packet parser this project carries from smoke-x-receiver (MIT © G-Two), with thanks.

The bridge talks to your phone and to nothing else. There is no cloud service, no account, and no telemetry — which is also why it keeps working when the internet does not.
